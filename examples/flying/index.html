    flying

    Open in the player, with the keyboard and gamepad reference.

    The flying tutorial, in Go.

    A port of L. Evans's Net Yaroze tutorial "flying" (Sony, 1996), the eighteenth in the "3-D object manipulation" series: a ship flying over a plane of tiles under a field of stars, with enemy ships that turn to face it and shoot. One enemy at a time appears somewhere on the plane, either sitting and tracking or giving chase, and goes down after ten hits in a burst of spinning sprites, for a score. A scanner in the corner shows where it is, pulsing. The view follows the ship from any of nine places, from close behind to high above to either side. The library's font prints the ship's position and the score, and the star field slides as the ship turns.

    Up and down move the ship forwards and back, Square and Circle turn it, L2 fires, R1 and R2 step through the views and L1 goes back to the first. Holding Select moves the viewpoint instead, by the directional buttons, Triangle and Cross, and R1 and R2 change the projection; Select and L2 step slowly. Start pauses, and Start and Select together quit. When the ship runs out of health, the score is shown until Start is pressed, and the program ends.

    Differences from the original:

    • The data is read off the disc to the addresses the original's batch script sent it to. The cube and sphere models the script also sends, which the program never uses, are left out.
    • After the score is shown, the program returns to the BIOS. The original goes on to a reset that starts with a failing assertion, which prints a message to the serial console and exits.
    • The bouncing spheres, which the original has switched off, and the level changes, which nothing in it reaches, are left out, and so are its assertions and what it prints to the serial console.
